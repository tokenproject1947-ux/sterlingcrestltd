Prioritize your savings goals based on what you really want

Savings goals work best when they reflect what you actually want — not what you think you should want. Prioritising them properly turns a vague wish to “save more” into a plan you can follow.

Write the goals down, with numbers

“Save for a home” is a wish. “Save 20,000 for a deposit by next December” is a goal. Give each goal an amount and a date and it becomes something you can plan around rather than worry about.

Protect the foundation first

Before the exciting goals, fund the unglamorous one: an emergency fund covering a few months of essential expenses. It is the difference between a setback and a crisis, and it keeps your other goals safe from being raided.

Rank by meaning, not by size

Order your goals by how much they matter to you. A smaller goal that genuinely excites you deserves funding before a large one that feels like an obligation. Momentum from reaching meaningful goals keeps you going toward the harder ones.

Automate and review

Automate transfers on payday so the decision is already made, and review your priorities a couple of times a year. Goals change as life changes — your plan should too.
